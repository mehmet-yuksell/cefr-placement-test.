### Yanlış yanıtladığınız sorular
_Açıklamalar İngilizcedir._
- **A1-ERR-001**: We [are] from Spain, but now we [live] [in] [the] Paris.
  - Yanıtınız: live
  - Doğru yanıt: the
  - City names normally take no article, so 'the Paris' is wrong; say 'in Paris'. 'Are' is correct with 'we'. 'Live' is the correct present simple for where you live now. 'In' is the right preposition for cities.
- **A2-RDG-002** (A message from Ella): When does Ella want to meet Sam?
  - Yanıtınız: On Saturday
  - Doğru yanıt: On Tuesday next week
  - Ella writes, 'Can we meet next week instead? I'm free on Tuesday after work.' Saturday is the day of Sam's party, Friday evening is when her family leaves, and Sunday afternoon is when they come back.
- **B1-LEX-001**: My doctor told me to ___ smoking, and I haven't had a cigarette since.
  - Yanıtınız: give out
  - Doğru yanıt: give up
  - 'Give up' means stop a habit. 'Give in' means accept defeat or agree after pressure, and it does not take an object like 'smoking'. 'Give out' means distribute. 'Give away' means give something to others for free.
- **B1-CLZ-002**: It's raining ___, so take an umbrella and a warm coat.
  - Yanıtınız: hardly
  - Doğru yanıt: heavily
  - 'Rain heavily' is the natural collocation. 'Strongly' is used with wind or opinions, not rain. 'Hardly' means 'almost not', the opposite of the intended meaning. 'Highly' means 'very' before some adjectives, as in 'highly likely'.
- **B1-CLZ-003**: This bridge ___ in 1890 and is still used today.
  - Yanıtınız: was building
  - Doğru yanıt: was built
  - The bridge did not build itself, so we need the passive 'was built'. 'Built' alone makes the bridge the builder. 'Has built' is an active present perfect. 'Was building' is an active continuous form, and the date shows a finished event.
- **A2-LEX-001**: It's cold outside, so ___ your jacket before you go.
  - Yanıtınız: get on
  - Doğru yanıt: put on
  - 'Put on' means start wearing clothes. 'Take off' is the opposite: remove clothes. 'Put off' means delay something. 'Get on' is used for buses and trains, or for relationships, not for clothes.
- **A2-LEX-002**: You are in a clothes shop and want to try on a sweater. What do you say to the shop assistant?
  - Yanıtınız: Do you try this on?
  - Doğru yanıt: Can I try this on, please?
  - 'Can I ... please?' is the normal polite way to ask. 'I want ... now' sounds like an order. 'You must' is too strong when you ask for permission. 'Do you try this on?' asks about the assistant, not about you.
- **A2-CLZ-002**: I can't hear the music. Can you make it ___, please?
  - Yanıtınız: stronger
  - Doğru yanıt: louder
  - We use 'louder' for the volume of a sound. 'Higher' describes the pitch of a note, not its volume. 'Stronger' is used for things like coffee or wind. 'Bigger' describes size, not sound.
- **A2-LEX-003**: Could you ___ me your pen for a minute? I'll give it back.
  - Yanıtınız: borrow
  - Doğru yanıt: lend
  - 'Lend' means let someone use something of yours for a short time. 'Borrow' is what the other person does: 'Can I borrow your pen?'. 'Keep' means not give it back. 'Take' does not fit the pattern 'take me something'.
- **A2-ERR-002**: Which sentence is correct?
  - Yanıtınız: I enjoy to play tennis on Sundays.
  - Doğru yanıt: I enjoy playing tennis on Sundays.
  - 'Enjoy' is followed by the -ing form, as in 'enjoy playing'. 'Enjoy to play' wrongly uses 'to' + verb, 'enjoy play' uses the base form, and 'am enjoy' adds 'am' before a present simple verb.
- **A2-CLZ-001**: Yesterday I ___ my keys, so I couldn't open the front door.
  - Yanıtınız: lose
  - Doğru yanıt: lost
  - 'Yesterday' is a finished time, so we use the past simple 'lost'. 'Have lost' is present perfect, which is not used with a finished time. 'Was losing' describes an action in progress, not a single event. 'Lose' is a present form.
- **A1-RDG-001** (Lena's week): Why does Lena like her job?
  - Yanıtınız: She starts work early.
  - Doğru yanıt: She meets a lot of people.
  - Lena says she likes her job 'because I meet a lot of people'. She does start early, but that is not the reason. The sea is near her grandmother's house, and she reads at home on Sundays.
- **A1-CLZ-002**: The shop is ___ now. It opens again at nine tomorrow morning.
  - Yanıtınız: full
  - Doğru yanıt: closed
  - 'Closed' means not open, so it matches a shop that opens again tomorrow. 'Close' as an adjective means 'near'. 'Open' contradicts 'opens again'. 'Full' means it has many people in it; it does not explain why it opens tomorrow.
- **B1-RDG-003** (Library notice): What is the main purpose of the notice?
  - Yanıtınız: To explain why the library is closing
  - Doğru yanıt: To tell people about changes at the library
  - The notice describes several changes: Sunday opening, shorter Wednesday hours and a new club. The survey has already happened. The library is not closing; it closes an hour earlier on one day. The reading club is for teenagers, not adults.

- **B1-CLZ-002**: It's raining ___, so take an umbrella and a warm coat.
  - Yanıtınız: hardly
  - Doğru yanıt: heavily

- **B2-ERR-002**: Which sentence is correct?
  - Yanıtınız: I wonder that why she didn't come to the meeting.
  - Doğru yanıt: I wonder why she didn't come to the meeting.
  - In an indirect question the word order is like a statement: 'why she didn't come'. Question order ('why didn't she') is wrong here. 'That' cannot be added before 'why'. The present perfect cannot be used with 'yesterday'.
- **B2-LEX-002**: The exam was a ___ of cake. I finished it in half the time.
  - Yanıtınız: bit
  - Doğru yanıt: piece
  - 'A piece of cake' is a fixed expression meaning 'very easy', and its words cannot be changed. 'A slice of cake' and 'a bit of cake' only describe real cake. 'Part' does not appear in this expression.
- **B2-CLZ-001**: Sara isn't answering her calls. She ___ her phone at home.
  - Yanıtınız: should have left
  - Doğru yanıt: must have left
  - 'Must have left' expresses a confident guess about the past. 'Must leave' refers to an obligation or to the present. 'Should have left' means it was the right thing to do, not a guess. 'Can have left' is not used for positive guesses about the past.
- **C2-ERR-002**: Which sentence is correct?
  - Yanıtınız: What surprised me most was how calm did she remain.
  - Doğru yanıt: What surprised me most was how calm she remained.
  - In this cleft sentence 'What' is the subject of 'surprised', and the clause after 'how calm' keeps statement order. 'How calm did she remain' wrongly uses question order. 'What it surprised' adds an extra subject. 'That' cannot replace 'What' at the start of this structure.
- **B1-RDG-004** (Library notice): Why will the library close earlier on Wednesdays?
  - Yanıtınız: Because few people visit then
  - Doğru yanıt: To pay for opening on Sundays
  - The notice says, 'To pay for the extra day, the library will close one hour earlier on Wednesdays.' It does not say Wednesdays are quiet, and the reading club and the book box are separate pieces of news.
- **A2-ERR-002**: Which sentence is correct?
  - Yanıtınız: I enjoy to play tennis on Sundays.
  - Doğru yanıt: I enjoy playing tennis on Sundays.
  - 'Enjoy' is followed by the -ing form, as in 'enjoy playing'. 'Enjoy to play' wrongly uses 'to' + verb, 'enjoy play' uses the base form, and 'am enjoy' adds 'am' before a present simple verb.

### Yanlış yanıtladığınız sorular
_Açıklamalar İngilizcedir._
- **B2-RDG-002** (Gardens between buildings): What does the word 'plots' mean in the text?
  - Yanıtınız: secret plans made by a group
  - Doğru yanıt: areas of land for growing plants
  - 'Plots' are the small areas of land in the garden, which are 'abandoned' when volunteers leave. 'Plot' can also mean a secret plan or the events of a story, but those meanings do not fit here. The volunteers are people, not the plots.
- **C1-CLZ-004**: The committee's decision was greeted with widespread ___ by local people.
  - Yanıtınız: unapproval
  - Doğru yanıt: disapproval
  - After 'widespread' we need a noun, and the negative noun of 'approval' is 'disapproval'. 'Disapproving' is an adjective, 'disapproved' is a verb form, and 'unapproval' is not an English word: 'approve' takes the prefix dis-, not un-.
- **B2-ERR-001**: I [had] my car [repair] last week, and it [has been running] [perfectly] since then.
  - Yanıtınız: has been running
  - Doğru yanıt: repair
  - In the causative 'have something done', the verb is a past participle: 'had my car repaired'. 'Had' is correct for last week. 'Has been running' fits an activity from then until now. 'Perfectly' is an adverb that correctly describes 'running'.
- **C1-CLZ-002**: Her argument doesn't ___ water: the figures she quotes are ten years old.
  - Yanıtınız: keep
  - Doğru yanıt: hold
  - 'Not hold water' is an idiom meaning that an argument or explanation is not convincing. 'Carry' is a blend with 'carry weight', a different idiom. 'Keep' and 'bear' do not form this expression, although both can mean 'hold' in other contexts.
- **C1-ERR-003**: Her research [focuses] [in] the effects of noise [on] sleep, a topic [few] people consider.
  - Yanıtınız: focuses
  - Doğru yanıt: in
  - 'Focus' takes 'on', so 'focuses in' should be 'focuses on'. 'Focuses' agrees with 'her research'. 'On' is correct in 'the effects of noise on sleep'. 'Few' correctly means 'not many', which fits a topic that people seldom think about.
